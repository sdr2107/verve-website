Body

The readings that come from a cuff, a tape measure and a blood draw, in the order the Body page shows them. Each one here carries the band Verve screens against and how often it is worth repeating.

These are screening bands, not treatment targets. What counts as a good number for you depends on your overall risk, and that is a conversation with your doctor. Verve classifies against population bands and says which study drew each line.

Where ApoB and LDL disagree, ApoB is the better read: it counts every atherogenic particle, which is what LDL alone can miss in insulin resistance and high triglycerides. Lp(a) barely moves through life, so one measurement usually settles it.

Intrinsic capacity

The frame these readings sit inside, and the score Verve will not build

The World Health Organization describes ageing well in five domains: moving, thinking, seeing and hearing, mood, and vitality. Verve measures two of them properly, movement and vitality, and says so. It will not add them into a single capacity score, because no such score is validated and a made-up total would bury the readings that are real.

Seven of the nine intervals are a guideline's. Two are ours, and say so.

Every band on this page comes from the app's own thresholds file, which carries the study behind each one. The repeat column now does the same. Seven are lifted straight from a guideline: HbA1c and fasting glucose from the ADA's screening interval, blood pressure from AHA/ACC 2017, and ApoB, LDL, triglycerides and HDL from one lipid panel on the AHA's four-to-six-year screening cycle, tightened to eight weeks after a treatment change.

Two have no guideline interval, and the page does not invent one. The Endocrine Society says a calculated insulin-resistance index belongs in research, not the clinic, so HOMA-IR carries no schedule at all and simply redraws when either of its inputs does. Neither European nor Asian sarcopenia group names a repeat interval for lean mass, so the six months shown is Verve's own, anchored to the six-month line EWGSOP2 draws between acute and chronic muscle loss. Both are labelled as ours in grey rather than dressed up as evidence.

One value inside a sourced row is still ours. The blood pressure guideline writes its rechecking advice around normal, elevated and stage 1, and gives its one-month figure for someone who has just started treatment rather than for an untreated reading at 140/90. Rather than leave a reading that high on the three-month interval, Verve reuses the guideline's own shortest cadence and marks it as our call.

These intervals now live in data/met-thresholds.json under retestIntervals, one entry per marker carrying the interval, the condition that shortens it, whether the number is a guideline's or ours, and the source string every other entry already carries. The days are indexed by the same severity function that paints the section dots, so a reading cannot colour one way and schedule another.
