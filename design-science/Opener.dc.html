“50 citations across 12 topics. No proprietary score you can't check the math on — every number below is the same one used inside the app, and every number links to where it came from.”

The counts are wrong. There are 61 citations and 20 topics on the page as it stands, and both were typed by hand, so they drift every time a study is added.

“Check the math on” picks a fight with competitors in the first ten words. The reader has not been told what this is yet.

Three claims in one sentence: a count, a negative, and a promise. The promise is the good one and it arrives last.

1 DRAWN INTO THE HUB

Verve shows you numbers a clinician would recognise, and tells you which study each one came from. Nothing is scored by a formula we will not show you.

The counts move out of the sentence and become three figures under it, counted in the build rather than typed: 61 citations, 20 topics, 0 made-up scores. The zero does the work the old negative was trying to do, without the elbow.

2 SHORTER, IF YOU WANT THE STATS GONE

Every number in Verve comes from a study, and every study is named here.

One sentence, no counts at all. Works if the three figures below feel like clutter; loses the chance to say how much evidence there is.

3 IF YOU WANT THE EDGE KEPT

No readiness score. No recovery percentage. No number we made up. Every reading here is one a clinician uses, and every one names its study.

Keeps the fight but aims it at the practice rather than at a rival. Good as a post; harder on a first-time visitor who has not met the category yet.
