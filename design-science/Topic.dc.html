Weekly volume

How much per week, and why the summit sits well above 150 minutes

In the app

The Activity page draws your week as a climb, with flags at 150, 300 and 560 minutes. The line is your aerobic minutes; the flags are where the evidence below changes what it says.

The World Health Organization's floor is 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, or 75 vigorous. That is a floor, not a target: the risk curve keeps falling well past it. In 2026 the British Journal of Sports Medicine put the flattening point at roughly 560 to 610 measured minutes a week, where the reduction in heart attack, stroke, heart failure and atrial fibrillation reaches about 30 percent, against 8 to 9 percent at 150.

Verve plants flags at all three and no further. Past the last one the climb carries on being drawn, but no flag is planted, because the evidence stops promising more.

WHO Guidelines on Physical Activity, 2020

150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week for adults, or 75 to 150 vigorous, plus strength work on two days.
