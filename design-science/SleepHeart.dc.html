Sleep timing

How much your bedtime moves, and the research index behind it

In the app

Three numbers: last night's hours, how far your bedtime drifts from one night to the next, and a regularity score from 0 to 100. Drift needs five nights. The score needs five comparable days, and a day is only comparable once there is a day before it to compare with, so five days means six nights.

REGULARITY
0 71.6 87.3 100

The marker is the UK Biobank median, 81.0. Bands are percentiles of that sample, not lines a guideline drew.

The index asks how likely you are to be in the same state, asleep or awake, at the same clock time on two consecutive days. It measures consistency, not duration: you can sleep seven hours a night and still score poorly if you take them at a different time each night. Bedtime drift answers the same question in minutes, which is the form a guideline uses and the form you can act on. Sixty minutes is the mark Verve draws.

The tile takes whichever of the two reads worse. Below your usual is never coloured, because a rested morning and an endurance athlete both land there. A rise has many ordinary causes before it has a worrying one: alcohol, heat, a late meal, poor sleep, altitude, hard training. Verve reports the deviation and its direction, and does not diagnose.

Heart rate recovery

One cited line, and why there is no second one

THE FALL IN THE FIRST MINUTE, BPM
0 12 40

Below the line is a cited finding. Above it is a gradient, not a grade: published cut-points span 12 to 30 depending on the study and the protocol, so Verve shades and refuses to print a verdict word.

A fall of twelve beats or fewer carried about four times the risk of death in 2,428 adults followed for six years, independent of workload and of what the ECG showed. A cohort in 2025 landed on the same line. It is not a proxy for blocked arteries: recovery predicted death even after accounting for what angiography showed, which is why it sits beside resting heart rate rather than inside a heart-disease panel.

How far your rate falls also depends on what you do after you stop. Walking it off and sitting down give different numbers, and the twelve-beat line was drawn with an active cool-down. Your watch measures whatever you happen to do, which is one more reason the comparison Verve leads with is you against your own recent readings.

Sleep and training

What the Guide's sleep line is, and what it refuses to claim

Because sleep and training now come from the same weekly summary, the Guide can lay them side by side: your average night, the minutes awake in it, and what you did in the days around it. It reports what moved together. It does not tell you that one caused the other, because a week of your own data cannot show that.
